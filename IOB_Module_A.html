1KYC / AML / CFT Policy
▸

Overview

Policy on Know Your Customer (KYC) / Anti-Money Laundering (AML) / Combating of Financing of Terrorism (CFT).

  • Board approval: 18.02.2025, valid upto 31.10.2026.
  • Addendum: 14.07.2026.
  • Based on RBI Master Directions.
  • Legal basis: Sec 35A of Banking Regulation Act, 1949 + Sec 45JA, 45K, 45L of RBI Act 1934 + Payment & Settlement Systems Act 2007 + FEMA 1999 + PMLA Rules 2005.
  • Applies to: every RBI-regulated entity; all home + overseas branches.
  • In case of conflict between RBI and Host country regulations: more stringent applies.

Objectives

  • Prevent misuse of banking channels for ML (Money Laundering) / TF (Terrorist Financing).
  • Guard against criminals, fraudsters, terrorists.
  • Understand risk per account/transaction.
  • Identify customer fully — identity, location, activity.
  • Implement FATF (Financial Action Task Force) + Basel Committee standards.

Key Definitions

  • CDD (Customer Due Diligence): Identify + verify customer and beneficial owner.
  • Customer Identification: Process of undertaking CDD.
  • Non-face-to-face customers: Open accounts without visiting branch/meeting officials.
  • On-going Due Diligence: Regular monitoring of transactions.
  • Periodic Updation: Keep KYC records current.
  • V-CIP (Video-based CIP): Live audio-visual, facial recognition — treated on par with face-to-face.
  • Digital KYC: Live photo + OVD + geo-location by authorised officer.
  • CKYCR: Central KYC Records Registry (14-digit for individuals, 15-digit for legal entities).
  • Equivalent e-document: Electronic doc with valid digital signature or from DigiLocker.

4 Key Elements of KYC Policy

  1. Customer Acceptance Policy (CAP)
  2. Risk Management
  3. Customer Identification Procedures (CIP)
  4. Monitoring of Transactions

Customer Acceptance Policy

  • Comprehensive info at initial stage. Interview by BM/Officer.
  • No anonymous / fictitious accounts allowed.
  • No account if CDD not possible (non-cooperation / unreliable documents).
  • CDD applied at UCIC (Unique Customer Identification Code) level.
  • Fresh CDD not needed for an existing KYC-compliant customer opening another account.
  • PAN / e-document — verified from issuing authority.
  • GST number — verified from search facility.
  • If bank suspects ML/TF and CDD may tip off customer — file STR with FIU-IND.

Officially Valid Documents (OVDs)

  • Passport
  • Driving Licence
  • Voter ID Card
  • PAN Card (now permissible as OVD with address proof)
  • Aadhaar (with proof of possession)
  • NREGA Job Card signed by State officer
  • Letter from National Population Register (NPR) with name & address

Deemed OVDs (address mismatch)

Where OVD has outdated address, the following are "deemed OVDs" for current address proof:

  • Utility bill (electricity, telephone, post-paid mobile, piped gas, water) not older than 2 months
  • Property tax receipt
  • Pension/family pension payment orders (PPOs) with address
  • Letter of allotment of accommodation from employer (Govt / PSU / scheduled bank / listed companies)

Must submit OVD with current address within 3 months.

Record Retention

  • Transaction records: 5 years from date of transaction.
  • KYC records: 5 years from date of cessation of business relationship or closure of account, whichever is later.

Simplified Measures

  • Small Account: balance ≤ ₹50,000 at any time; credits ≤ ₹1 lakh/year; withdrawals ≤ ₹10,000/month. No OVD needed; self-attested photograph + signature.
  • Opened subject to monitoring; valid 12 months (extendable 12 more with OVD application pending).

PEP (Politically Exposed Persons)

  • Individuals entrusted with prominent public functions in foreign countries.
  • Enhanced due diligence by senior management.
  • Source of funds + wealth to be verified.
  • Closely followed — periodic KYC updation every 2 years.
  • Relatives + close associates of PEPs — treated similarly.
2Central KYC Records Registry (CKYCR)
▸
  • Under Rule 2(1) of PMLA Rules. Receives, stores, safeguards, retrieves KYC records digitally.
  • CERSAI (Central Registry of Securitisation Asset Reconstruction) is the agency managing CKYCR.
  • KYC Identifier: Unique 14-digit number (individuals) / 15-digit (legal entities).
  • Bank uploads KYC data within 10 days of account opening / updation.
  • Any KYC-compliant customer with CKYC number — can open account in any RE without physical KYC.
  • Registered Investment Advisers, NBFCs, Insurance Cos, Mutual Funds — all pool KYC via CKYCR.
  • Fetching KYC via CKYC Identifier reduces duplication.
Benefit: Customer completes KYC once; usable across all regulated financial institutions.

Who can open account?

  • HUF, Partnership Firm, LLP, Company, Trust, Society, Association, Govt/Semi-Govt Dept, Co-op Society.

Banking Regulation Act, 1949

  • Sec 5(b): Banking = accepting deposits of money from public, for lending/investment, repayable on demand or otherwise, withdrawable by cheque/draft/order.
  • Sec 7: Use of word "bank" — only allowed to RBI-licensed entities.
  • Sec 20: Restriction on loans to directors.
  • Sec 21: RBI power to determine policy of banks.
  • Sec 22: License from RBI mandatory.
  • Sec 24: SLR maintenance.
  • Sec 29: Accounts and Balance Sheet.
  • Sec 35: Inspection by RBI.
  • Sec 35A: Power to issue directions.
  • Sec 45ZA-ZF: Nomination facility for deposits/lockers/articles.

RBI Act, 1934

  • Sec 42(1): CRR maintenance.
  • Sec 45JA, 45K, 45L: Regulation of NBFCs.
  • Sec 20: Issue of Bank Notes.

Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA) 2002

  • Offence: proceeds of crime + schedule offences.
  • Punishment: 3 to 7 years imprisonment + fine (up to 10 years for drug-related).
  • Adjudicating Authority — attachment of property.
4Rights of Banker
▸

1. Right of General Lien (Sec 171, Indian Contract Act)

  • Right to retain securities/property belonging to customer against general debt.
  • Applies to all securities (goods, documents) that come lawfully into bank's possession in course of business.
  • Doesn't apply to: safe custody articles, safe deposit locker contents, specific-purpose securities.

2. Right of Set-Off

  • Right to combine two or more accounts of same customer in same capacity.
  • Conditions:
    • Debts must be mutual.
    • Same capacity (SB + CA personal — yes; personal SB + partnership account — no).
    • Debts must be matured (for customer side).
    • Notice to customer preferred.

3. Right of Appropriation (Clayton's Rule — Devaynes v. Noble)

  • In running accounts (CC/OD): First credit discharges first debit (FIFO).
  • Customer can appropriate at time of payment.
  • If customer doesn't — banker appropriates (notify).
  • If neither — Court applies Clayton's rule.

4. Right to Charge Interest, Commission, Service Charges

  • By implied agreement (customary).
  • Rates as per RBI guidelines + bank policy.

5. Right to Close Account

  • After reasonable notice to customer.
  • Grounds: suspected ML/TF, adverse customer behaviour, continuing fraud.

Crossing

  • General Crossing (Sec 123): Two parallel transverse lines with/without words "& Co.", "Not Negotiable".
  • Special Crossing (Sec 124): Bank's name between two lines.
  • Account Payee Crossing: Must be credited to that account only.
  • Not Negotiable (Sec 130): Transferee gets no better title than transferor.

Endorsement Types

  • Blank: Only signature.
  • Full / Special: Signature + name of transferee.
  • Restrictive: "Pay X only" — limits further negotiation.
  • Partial: Not valid.
  • Conditional: Valid between parties but bank can ignore.

Cheque — Specific Rules

  • Validity: 3 months from date (earlier 6 months).
  • Post-dated / Ante-dated / Stale cheque — treatment specific.
  • Material alteration — authenticated with full signature.
  • Mutilated cheque — needs confirmation.
  • Minor can be a drawer (Sec 26) — binds other parties but not himself.

Sec 138 — Dishonour for Insufficient Funds

  1. Cheque presented within 3 months of date.
  2. Returned unpaid for insufficient funds / exceeds arrangement.
  3. Payee issues demand notice within 30 days of return memo.
  4. Drawer's payment time: 15 days of receipt.
  5. Complaint filed within 1 month of failure to pay.
  6. Punishment: Imprisonment up to 2 years + fine up to 2× cheque amount.

Key Rules

  • Safe Deposit Locker: Multiple survivors permitted (jointly operated locker).

  • Nominee is only trustee for legal heirs.
  • Nominee's rights are subject to Succession Laws.

8Special Types of Customers
▸

Minor

  • Below 18 years (21 if guardian appointed by Court).
  • Self-operated account: Age 10+, SB only, balance limit as per bank policy (typically ₹2 lakh now; was ₹2,500 historically — follow latest circular).
  • Natural guardian: Father (then Mother); Court-appointed guardian for orphan.
  • Mother as natural guardian (Hindu Minority Act): if father is alive & in India, Mother is NOT natural guardian — except for illegitimate child.
  • Minor can have TD jointly with guardian.
  • No cheque book for self-operated under 10.

Illiterate

  • No cheque book.
  • Thumb impression + 2 witnesses.
  • Introducer required.
  • Photo on passbook.
  • For withdrawals — physical presence + thumb impression.

Partnership Firm

  • Partnership Deed + Registration Certificate (preferred).
  • Max partners: 50 (except professional firms).
  • Minor can be admitted to benefits (not as full partner).
  • Mandate signed by all partners.

Companies

  • Certificate of Incorporation (CoI).
  • Memorandum of Association (MOA) + Articles of Association (AOA).
  • Board Resolution authorising account opening + operating signatories.
  • PAN, GST, Udyam (if MSME).
  • Max members: Private 200; Public — no max.

Hindu Undivided Family (HUF)

  • Karta operates account.
  • Letter of declaration by Karta + coparceners.
  • HUF PAN (separate from Karta).
  • On Karta's death, eldest male/female coparcener becomes Karta.

Trust

  • Trust Deed; 12A / 80G registration if applicable.
  • Trustees operate jointly / per Deed.
  • Public Trust / Private Trust / Charitable Trust.

Pardanashin Lady

  • Introduced by respectable person known to Bank.
  • Witness required.
  • Thumb impression / signature verification carefully.

Blind Person

  • Can operate SB/TD/Locker — RBI mandate.
  • Thumb impression with witness.
  • Transactions during business hours only.

Mentally Challenged / Insolvent

  • Lunatic: Manager appointed by Court; mandate from Court.
  • Insolvent: Official Assignee takes over.

Executor / Administrator

  • Executor: appointed by Will (needs Probate).
  • Administrator: appointed by Court (Letter of Administration).
  • Account in name "A, Executor/Administrator of late B."

Joint Accounts

  • Either or Survivor (E/S): Any one; on death, survivor operates.
  • Former or Survivor (F/S): First holder only; on death, second operates.
  • Jointly: All must sign.
  • Jointly or Survivor: Together during life; survivor after death of one.

NRI

  • Indian citizen residing abroad ≥ 182 days in prev FY.
  • Account types: NRE, NRO, FCNR(B).
  • On return to India: convert NRE/FCNR → RFC.

10DEAF Scheme 2014
▸

Depositor Education and Awareness Fund (DEAF) Scheme, 2014 under Sec 26A of BR Act.

  • Unclaimed / inoperative deposits for 10 years → transferred to RBI's DEAF.
  • Transfer by last working day of the month succeeding the month of 10 years.
  • Interest paid by RBI: 3% p.a. (revised from time to time).
  • Customer can still claim — bank refunds customer & claims refund from RBI.
  • Purpose: DEAF used for depositor education & awareness programmes.
  • Includes: SB, CA, FD, RD balances; dividend warrants; drafts; pay orders — unclaimed for 10 years.
11DICGC
▸

Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (DICGC) — subsidiary of RBI, established under DICGC Act, 1961.

Coverage

  • ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank (principal + interest).
  • Covers: SB, CA, FD, RD.
  • Excludes: Govt deposits, Inter-bank deposits, Foreign Govt deposits, State Govt deposits (land dev banks).

Premium

  • Paid by Bank (not customer).
  • Rate: 12 paise per ₹100 of assessable deposits per annum.
  • Half-yearly payment — April & October.

Settlement

  • Pay within 90 days of imposition of moratorium / bank failure (as per 2021 amendment).
  • Earlier: pay on liquidation.
  • Separate ₹5 lakh for same depositor in different banks.
  • Same depositor in different capacity in same bank — separate ₹5 lakh.

Multiple Capacity Examples

  • Mr. X — Individual + Partner in firm = Different capacities (2 × ₹5L).
  • Mr. X + Mrs. X joint = Different from Mr. X individual.
  • Minor through guardian = Minor's capacity.

  2. Right to Transparency, Fair & Honest Dealing.
  3. Right to Suitability.
  4. Right to Privacy.
  5. Right to Grievance Redress and Compensation.

Compensation for Service Deficiencies

  • Delay in cheque collection — specific slabs.
  • Wrong debit — reverse + compensation.
  • Unauthorized electronic transactions — zero/limited/full liability framework.

16Integrated Ombudsman Scheme 2021
▸

"One Nation, One Ombudsman" — notified by RBI on 12.11.2021 under Sec 35A of BR Act.

Coverage

  • Scheduled Commercial Banks.
  • Regional Rural Banks.
  • Primary (Urban) Co-op Banks.
  • NBFCs (eligible).
  • Payment System Participants.
  • Pre-paid Payment Instruments (PPIs).

Who can file?

  • Any customer (individual/entity).
  • After 30 days of no satisfactory reply from bank.
  • Within 1 year of reply (or 1 year 30 days from complaint).

Compensation Limits

  • Direct loss: up to ₹20 lakh.
  • Indirect loss (loss of time, expenses, harassment, mental agony): up to ₹1 lakh.

Procedure

  • Online complaint at cms.rbi.org.in
  • Helpline: 14448
  • Postal address (any of 22 Ombudsman offices).
  • Mediation preferred — if unresolved, award.
  • Appeal to Appellate Authority (Deputy Governor RBI) within 30 days.

Grounds for Complaint

  • Non-adherence to RBI guidelines.
  • Service deficiency — any banking service.
  • Delay / non-payment of cheques, drafts, DDs.
  • Unfair / discriminatory treatment.
17Internal Ombudsman Scheme 2026
▸

Applicability

  • All Scheduled Commercial Banks (except RRBs).
  • NBFC-ND-SI with ≥ 10 branches.
  • Payment System Operators (threshold).

Role

  • Review complaints partially or wholly rejected by the bank.
  • Independent of bank management.
  • Appointed from outside: retired General Manager / CGM of scheduled commercial bank with 7 years' exp.

Features

  • Tenure: 3 years, extendable to 5 years.
  • Cannot be removed arbitrarily — Board approval required.
  • Reports to Managing Director / CEO administratively; functionally independent.
  • Quarterly report to Customer Service Committee of Board.

Workflow

  • Complaint rejected by bank → automatically goes to IO before informing customer.
  • IO reviews within 20 days.
  • If IO upholds rejection, customer can approach RBI Ombudsman.
18Garnishee Order
▸

Court order under Order 21, Rule 46 of CPC — attaching debt owed by garnishee (bank) to judgment debtor (customer).

Two Stages

  1. Order Nisi: Provisional order, directing bank not to pay customer; shows cause why absolute order should not be made.
  2. Order Absolute: After hearing, final order to pay to judgment creditor.

Applicability

  • Credit balance at time of receipt of order.
  • Not applicable to future credits.

Not Applicable To

  • Partnership account (for partner's debt).
  • Joint account (for one holder's debt) — unless specifically mentioned.
  • Trust account.
  • Cheques in course of collection.
  • Fixed Deposit (unmatured).
  • Minimum balance in SB (if scheme-specific exemption).

Bank's Course of Action

  1. Mark lien on balance.
  2. No notice to customer required under Nisi.
  3. On Absolute, remit to court / judgment creditor.

▸
  • Issued by: Income Tax Dept (Sec 226(3)), GST Dept, Central Excise, Enforcement Directorate, etc.

20Currency Management
▸

Clean Note Policy (RBI)

  • No stapling.
  • No writing on notes.
  • No pinning.
  • Store in loose bundles.

Note Categories

  • Clean/Fit: Suitable for recirculation.
  • Soiled: Dirty but intact — exchanged at any branch.
  • Mutilated: Missing portion — exchanged under RBI Note Refund Rules 2009.
  • Imperfect: Signs of wear, no essential feature missing.

Soiled Note Exchange

  • Free of cost.
  • Up to 20 pieces or ₹5,000 per day at counter (no slip).
  • Above: with slip + ID.

Note Refund Rules 2009

  • Mutilated notes — partial/full value based on area of each piece.
  • Essential features (RBI Govr sign, serial no, Ashok Pillar, promise clause, Gandhi portrait).

Coin Exchange

  • Coins of all denominations are legal tender.
  • ₹1, ₹2 up to ₹1,000 in a transaction (Sec 6, Coinage Act 2011).
  • ₹5+ coins — any amount.
21Counterfeit Note Detection
▸

On Detection

  1. Impound immediately.
  2. Issue acknowledgment to tenderer (receipt).
  3. Stamp "COUNTERFEIT BANK NOTE" (red ink, 5cm × 5cm).
  4. Make entry in Counterfeit Note Register.

Reporting

  • Up to 4 notes: Report monthly consolidated to Nodal Officer + Police.
  • 5+ notes in single transaction: Report to Police + Nodal Officer within 3 working days as per FIR.
  • CCR (Counterfeit Currency Report) to FIU-IND within 7 working days.

Compensation to Victim

  • No compensation payable by bank to tenderer.
  • If bank fails to impound: 4× face value as penalty by RBI.

Security Features of Current Notes

  • Watermark (Mahatma Gandhi).
  • Security thread.
  • Latent image.
  • Micro lettering.
  • Intaglio printing.
  • See-through register.
  • Fluorescence.
  • Optical variable ink (OVI).
  • Identification mark for visually impaired.
  • Year of printing on reverse.

26Goods and Service Tax (GST)
▸

Overview

  • Levied: 1 July 2017.
  • Destination-based tax.
  • Four components: CGST + SGST / UTGST + IGST (inter-state).
  • Banking services: 18% GST.

Registration Threshold

  • Goods: ₹40 lakh turnover (₹20 lakh for special category states).
  • Services: ₹20 lakh (₹10 lakh for special states).

GST-Exempt (Banking Services)

  • Interest on loans / deposits.
  • Discount on bills.
  • Sale/purchase of forex at interbank rate.
  • Services by RBI.

GST-Applicable (Banking)

  • Loan processing fees.
  • Locker rent.
  • SB non-maintenance charges.
  • Issuance of DD, PO.
  • SMS alerts, debit card AMC.
  • SB account charges (chequebook, etc.).
27CERSAI
▸

Central Registry of Securitisation Asset Reconstruction and Security Interest of India — under Sec 20 of SARFAESI Act 2002.

Purpose

  • Single registry of security interests created by banks/FIs.
  • Prevent multiple financing against same asset.
  • Public searchable database.

Registration

  • Timeline: within 30 days of creation of security interest.
  • Late registration permitted with additional fee.
  • Also: modification / satisfaction of charge.

Fees (indicative)

  • Loans ≤ ₹5 lakh: ₹50
  • Loans > ₹5 lakh: ₹100
  • Modification: ₹50 / ₹100
  • Search: ₹10 per transaction

Covered

  • Mortgages (equitable + registered).
  • Hypothecation of movables.
  • Assignment of receivables.
