1Money Market Instruments
▸

Certificate of Deposit (CD)

  • Issuer: Scheduled Commercial Banks (except RRBs, LABs) + specified AIFIs.
  • Minimum: ₹5 lakh; multiples of ₹5 lakh.
  • Tenure: Banks 7 days to 1 year; AIFIs 1 to 3 years.
  • Issued at discount; no premature repayment.
  • Negotiable instrument; transferable.
  • Not callable (except specific conditions).

Commercial Paper (CP)

  • Issuer: Companies / NBFCs with net worth ≥ ₹4 Cr.
  • Minimum rating: A2 / A3 (short-term credit rating).
  • Minimum: ₹5 lakh; multiples of ₹5 lakh.
  • Tenure: 7 days to 1 year.
  • Unsecured; issued at discount.
  • IPA (Issuing & Paying Agent) = Scheduled commercial bank.

Call Money / Notice Money / Term Money

  • Call: Up to 1 day (overnight).
  • Notice: 2-14 days.
  • Term: 15+ days up to 1 year.
  • Participants: Banks (both borrowers + lenders), Primary Dealers.

Treasury Bills (T-Bills)

  • Issued by GoI (via RBI).
  • Three types: 91-day, 182-day, 364-day.
  • Minimum: ₹25,000; multiples of ₹25,000.
  • Issued at discount; redeemed at face value.
2MCLR & External Benchmark Based Rate
▸

MCLR — Marginal Cost of Funds Based Lending Rate

  • Introduced: 1 April 2016 (replaced Base Rate).
  • Components:
    1. Marginal Cost of Funds (weighted avg of borrowing rates).
    2. Negative Carry on CRR.
    3. Operating Costs.
    4. Tenor Premium.
  • Tenors: Overnight, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year.
  • Review: Monthly.
  • Reset: Minimum every 3 months.

EBLR / RLLR — External Benchmark Based Rate

  • Effective 1 October 2019 for all floating rate retail + MSE loans.
  • Benchmarks allowed (RBI):
    • Policy Repo Rate.
    • Government of India 3-month Treasury Bill yield (FBIL).
    • Government of India 6-month Treasury Bill yield (FBIL).
    • Any other benchmark published by FBIL.
  • Reset: minimum every 3 months.
  • Most banks use Repo-Linked Lending Rate (RLLR) = Repo + Spread.
  • Spread = Credit risk premium + Operating cost + Bank margin.
3CRR & SLR Requirements
▸

CRR — Cash Reserve Ratio

  • Current: 4.00% of NDTL (Net Demand and Time Liabilities).
  • Maintained with RBI as cash balance.
  • No interest earned.
  • Calculated on fortnightly average basis.
  • Minimum daily maintenance: 95% of required.
  • Sec 42(1) of RBI Act 1934.

SLR — Statutory Liquidity Ratio

  • Current: 18.00% of NDTL.
  • Maintained in cash, gold, approved Govt securities.
  • Earns interest (on Govt securities).
  • Sec 24 of BR Act 1949.
  • Also fortnightly basis.

LAF — Liquidity Adjustment Facility

  • RBI's tool for day-to-day liquidity management.
  • Repo Rate: Rate at which banks borrow from RBI (short-term).
  • Current Repo: 5.50% (verify latest).
  • Reverse Repo: Replaced by Standing Deposit Facility (SDF).
  • SDF Rate: Repo - 25 bps.
  • MSF (Marginal Standing Facility): Repo + 25 bps (emergency).
  • Bank Rate: typically MSF rate.

Monetary Policy Framework

  • MPC (Monetary Policy Committee): 6 members — 3 RBI + 3 external.
  • Inflation targeting: 4% (±2% tolerance band).
  • Meeting frequency: at least once in 2 months.
4Treasury Operations
▸

Investment Classification (Pre Sep 2024)

CategoryFeatures
HTM (Held to Maturity)Intended till maturity; cost basis; no MTM
AFS (Available for Sale)Periodic MTM; MTM gain/loss to Investment Reserve
HFT (Held for Trading)Short-term trading; daily MTM to P&L

New Investment Categorisation (Sep 2024 RBI Directions)

  • HTM — permitted for SLR, equity in subsidiaries, etc.
  • AFS — Available for Sale.
  • FVTPL (Fair Value Through P&L) — replaces HFT; broader scope.
  • Investments in subsidiaries, JVs, associates — separate category.

Investment Fluctuation Reserve (IFR)

  • Minimum 2% of HFT + AFS portfolio.
  • Buffer against adverse price movements.
  • Appropriated from profit.

Dealers

  • Primary Dealers (PD): 21 PDs in India; deal in G-Secs directly with RBI.
  • Standalone PD vs Bank PD (subsidiary of bank).

Key Trading Platforms

  • CCIL (Clearing Corporation of India Ltd): Clearing + Settlement.
  • NDS (Negotiated Dealing System): Govt securities.
  • CROMS: Market borrowing operations.
  • CBLO: Collateralized Borrowing & Lending Obligation.
  • FBIL: Financial Benchmarks India Limited.
5Basel III Capital Framework
▸

Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR / CRAR)

  • RBI: 9% minimum (Basel III mandates 8%).
  • Formula: (Tier 1 + Tier 2) / RWA × 100.
  • RWA = Risk Weighted Assets.

Capital Components

ComponentRBI Requirement
CET1 (Common Equity Tier 1)5.5%
Additional Tier 1 (AT1)1.5%
Total Tier 17.0%
Tier 2 (max)2.0%
Total Capital (CAR)9.0%
Capital Conservation Buffer (CCB)2.5% above regulatory minimum
Countercyclical Buffer0-2.5%

Liquidity Standards

  • LCR (Liquidity Coverage Ratio): 100% — HQLA ≥ 30-day net cash outflow.
  • NSFR (Net Stable Funding Ratio): ≥ 100% — Available Stable Funding / Required Stable Funding.

Leverage Ratio

  • ≥ 3.5% for D-SIBs.
  • ≥ 4% for others.
  • Formula: Tier 1 / Total Exposure.

D-SIBs (Domestic Systemically Important Banks)

  • SBI, HDFC, ICICI (currently).
  • Higher capital requirements due to size/connectedness.
6Risk Management — Overview
▸

Types of Risk

  • Credit Risk: Default by borrower / counterparty.
  • Market Risk: Interest rate, forex, equity, commodity price movements.
  • Operational Risk: Fraud, process failure, system failure, external events.
  • Liquidity Risk: Asset-liability mismatch; funding shortfall.
  • Reputation Risk: Loss of trust, brand damage.
  • Strategic Risk: Business model errors.
  • Legal / Compliance Risk: Violation of laws, regulations.
  • Systemic Risk: Risk to entire financial system.

Credit Risk Management

  • Credit Policy + credit appraisal.
  • Rating models (internal + external).
  • Collateral management.
  • Diversification (sector, geography).
  • Monitoring + EWS.
  • Portfolio stress testing.

Market Risk Management

  • VaR (Value at Risk) — statistical measure.
  • Stress testing.
  • Scenario analysis.
  • Duration + convexity analysis.
  • Position limits.
  • Stop-loss limits.

Operational Risk Management

  • Risk Control Self-Assessment (RCSA).
  • Key Risk Indicators (KRIs).
  • Internal Loss Data.
  • Business Continuity Planning (BCP).
  • Disaster Recovery Planning.

Measurement Models (Basel)

  • Credit Risk: Standardised (CRAR), IRB (Foundation + Advanced).
  • Market Risk: Standardised, Internal Models (VaR-based).
  • Operational Risk: Basic Indicator, Standardised, Advanced Measurement.
7Asset Liability Management (ALM)
▸
  • Objective: Manage mismatch between assets and liabilities.
  • ALCO (Asset Liability Committee) — meets weekly/fortnightly.

Tools

  • Interest Rate Sensitivity Gap (IRS Gap): Rate-sensitive Assets - Rate-sensitive Liabilities.
  • Duration Gap: Weighted avg time to cash flow.
  • Dynamic Liquidity Statement.
  • Structural Liquidity Statement.
  • Interest Rate Sensitivity Report.

Tolerance Limits

  • Up to 14 days: Cumulative mismatch ≤ 5% of outflows.
  • 14-28 days: ≤ 10%.
  • Monitored by ALCO.
8Fraud Risk Management
▸

Fraud Categories (Classification)

  • Misappropriation + criminal breach of trust.
  • Fraudulent encashment through forged instruments.
  • Manipulation of books.
  • Cheating / Forgery.
  • Irregularities in advances (false documents, diversion).
  • Cash shortages.
  • Fraudulent remittances.
  • Cyber frauds.

Reporting to RBI

  • FMR-1: ≥ ₹1 Cr — within 21 days of detection.
  • FMR-2: Quarterly progress.
  • FMR-3: Annual review.
  • CFR (Central Fraud Registry) — RBI's central database.

EWS (Early Warning Signals) Framework

  • 45+ indicators of stress / fraud.
  • For accounts > ₹25 Cr typically.
  • Red Flagged Accounts (RFA) classification.
  • 6 months investigation → fraud / genuine stress.

Fraud Prevention

  • KYC / AML strict enforcement.
  • Segregation of duties (maker-checker).
  • Access controls.
  • Periodic rotation.
  • Whistleblower policy.
  • Training + awareness.
9ICON Rating (IOB)
▸
  • ICON = IOB Credit Rating Model (internal).
  • Ratings: ICON 1 (lowest risk) to ICON 10 (highest risk).
  • Parameters:
    • Financial: ratios, performance.
    • Management: experience, integrity.
    • Industry: dynamics, cycle.
    • Operational: capacity, utilization.
  • Used for:
    • Pricing (interest rate spread based on rating).
    • Sanction authority delegation.
    • Provisioning (migration-based).
    • Portfolio monitoring.
  • Rating migration tracked quarterly.
10Climate Risk
▸
  • RBI Framework 2024-onwards.
  • Climate Risk Questionnaire for credit proposals ≥ ₹100 Cr.
  • Assesses:
    • Physical risk (floods, droughts, cyclones impact on borrower).
    • Transition risk (carbon regulation, shift away from fossil).
    • Reputation risk.
    • Environmental impact.
  • Green Finance — separate framework.
  • Reporting to Board + RBI.
11Treasury Bills + Govt Securities
▸

T-Bills

  • 91-day, 182-day, 364-day.
  • Min: ₹25,000.
  • Zero coupon; issued at discount.

G-Secs

  • Dated Securities — 5 to 30 years.
  • Coupon-bearing semi-annually.
  • Issued by RBI via auction (On behalf of GoI).
  • Primary market: Weekly auctions.
  • Secondary market: NDS-OM.

SDLs (State Development Loans)

  • Issued by state governments.
  • Semi-annual coupon.
  • Riskier than Central G-Sec (though AAA by rating).

Bond Terminology

  • Coupon = Interest rate.
  • YTM (Yield to Maturity): Return till maturity.
  • Duration: Price sensitivity to interest rate change.
  • Modified Duration: Duration / (1 + YTM).
  • Convexity: 2nd-order price sensitivity.
  • Credit Spread: Corporate bond yield - G-Sec yield.
12Policy on Fraud Risk Management
▸
  • RBI Directions on Frauds (July 2024 revised).
  • Covers detection, reporting, investigation, action.

Timelines

  • Fraud detection → Classification: Within 7 days.
  • FMR-1 submission: Within 21 days.
  • Internal action: Within 90 days.
  • CRILC reporting: Daily.

Action Points

  • FIR with police.
  • CBI referral (for large frauds > ₹25 Cr).
  • Internal disciplinary action.
  • Staff accountability examination.
  • Recovery from fraudster.

Prevention Framework

  • Fraud Risk Management Committee (FRMC) at board level.
  • Fraud Monitoring Officer at Central Office.
  • Whistleblower protection.
  • Vigilance Department oversight.
13Interest Rate Policy 2026 (IOB)
▸
  • Reviewed annually; approved by Board.
  • Governs:
    • Deposit rates (SB, TD, specialised).
    • Lending rates (RLLR, MCLR-linked, specific schemes).
    • Non-funded (BG, LC) charges.
  • Rate changes communicated to branches with effect date.
  • Must be transparent + non-discriminatory.
  • Reset: minimum every 3 months.
14Monetary Policy Transmission
▸
  • RBI's challenge: Transmission of policy rate changes to lending/deposit rates.
  • Repo cut → RLLR cut → Lower EMI → Growth stimulus.
  • Repo hike → RLLR hike → Higher borrowing cost → Inflation control.

Why RLLR?

  • MCLR-based loans had slow transmission.
  • External benchmark forces immediate pass-through.
  • Still: Spread can change (not transparent).

Timelines

  • Benchmark change → EMI reset within 3 months.
  • Reset must be as per loan agreement.
