1Staff Matters
▸

Bipartite Settlement (BPS)

  • Periodic wage revision between IBA + Bank Unions.
  • Currently: 12th BPS (ongoing/recently concluded).
  • Covers workmen + officers.
  • Includes: salary structure, DA formula, HRA, LFC, perquisites.

IOBOSR (IOB Officer Service Regulations)

  • Governs service conditions of officers.
  • Covers: recruitment, promotion, transfer, pay scales, leave, LFC, retirement.

Leaves

Privilege Leave (PL)33 days/year (max accumulation 240 days)
Earned LeavePart of PL
Maternity Leave180 days (26 weeks)
Paternity Leave15 days
Study LeaveAs per policy
Extraordinary Leave (EOL)As per approval

LFC (Leave Fare Concession)

  • Once in 2 years (Short) / 4 years (Long).
  • Reimbursement of travel (self + family).
  • LTC also available to Govt employees — similar.

Pension & NPS

  • Officers joined after 01.04.2010: NPS mandatory.
  • Officers joined before 01.04.2010: Pension scheme eligible.
  • Family pension: dependent on service rules.

Transfer Policy

  • Scale-wise tenure at a station typically 3-5 years.
  • Tribal/difficult area posting: compensatory benefits.
  • Married couple: joint posting preferred.
2Companies Act 2013
▸

Replaces Companies Act 1956 — enacted August 2013. Enforced via MCA.

Key Types of Companies

TypeMin / Max Members
Private CompanyMin 2 / Max 200
Public CompanyMin 7 / No Max
OPC (One Person Company)1 member
Small CompanyPaid-up ≤ ₹4 Cr OR Turnover ≤ ₹40 Cr (revised)
LLPMin 2 partners; No Max (separate Act)

Key Documents

  • MOA (Memorandum of Association): Company's constitution; objectives, state of registered office, capital.
  • AOA (Articles of Association): Internal rules and bye-laws.
  • CIN (Corporate Identity Number): 21-digit unique identifier.
  • Certificate of Incorporation: Issued by RoC.

Key Sections

  • Sec 2(62): One Person Company.
  • Sec 2(68): Private Company definition.
  • Sec 2(71): Public Company definition.
  • Sec 2(87): Subsidiary Company.
  • Sec 73-76: Deposits.
  • Sec 77: Registration of Charge — within 30 days.
  • Sec 78: Registration of charge by charge-holder.
  • Sec 180: Board restrictions — no sale/lease of undertaking or borrowing > paid-up + free reserves without shareholder approval.
  • Sec 185: Loans to directors — generally prohibited (except conditions).
  • Sec 186: Loans and investments by company.
  • Sec 139-146: Audit provisions.
  • Sec 135: CSR.
  • Sec 447: Fraud punishment — imprisonment 6 months to 10 years + fine.

CSR (Sec 135)

  • Applicable if any of: Net worth ≥ ₹500 Cr OR Turnover ≥ ₹1000 Cr OR Net Profit ≥ ₹5 Cr (in any of preceding 3 FYs).
  • Spend: 2% of average net profit of 3 preceding FYs.
  • Schedule VII: permitted CSR activities.
  • Unspent amount: transfer to CSR Unspent Account / Unspent Fund.

Independent Directors

  • Mandatory for listed companies.
  • At least 1/3rd of Board as Independent.
  • Qualifications: Integrity + relevant expertise.
  • Databank maintained by IICA (Indian Institute of Corporate Affairs).

Committees Required

  • Audit Committee (Sec 177).
  • Nomination & Remuneration Committee (Sec 178).
  • CSR Committee (Sec 135).
  • Stakeholders Relationship Committee (listed).
3Registration of Charge on Co Asset
▸

Section 77, Companies Act 2013.

Timeline

  • Create / modify / satisfy charge → file with ROC within 30 days.
  • Extension: another 30 days (total 60 days) with additional fee.
  • Further extension (60 to 120 days): by Regional Director on condonation + higher fee.

Forms

FormPurpose
CHG-1Creation / Modification of charge
CHG-2Certificate of registration (issued by RoC)
CHG-4Satisfaction of charge
CHG-6Notice by charge-holder
CHG-8Application for extension/condonation
CHG-9Creation/modification of charge by debenture issue

Consequences of Non-Registration

  • Charge void against liquidator / creditors.
  • Charge still enforceable against borrower (Companies Act).
  • Bank's priority lost — ranks as unsecured.
  • Penalty on company + officers.

Priority

  • First registered charge has priority over subsequent.
  • Also relevant: CERSAI registration under SARFAESI.
4Letter of Guarantee (BG)
▸

Types

  • Financial Guarantee: Guarantee for payment of money.
  • Performance Guarantee: Guarantee for performance of contract.
  • Deferred Payment Guarantee (DPG): Guarantee for payment at future dates.

Common Sub-Types

  • Bid Bond (EMD) — tender participation.
  • Advance Payment Guarantee — advance paid to contractor.
  • Retention Money Guarantee — in lieu of retention amount.
  • Customs Duty Guarantee — for duty payment.
  • Earnest Money Deposit Guarantee.

Features

  • Margin: Typically 10-25% (based on risk, borrower rating).
  • Commission: 0.5% to 3% p.a. (based on tenure, risk).
  • Tenure: As per contract — typically 1-5 years.
  • Claim Period: Must be clearly defined (date beyond which claim becomes invalid).
  • Governed by ICC URDG 758 (international) + Contract Act (domestic).
  • Bank's obligation:
    • Financial Guarantee: unconditional, pay on first demand.
    • Performance Guarantee: conditional on default being established.

Invocation

  • Beneficiary issues invocation letter within validity.
  • Bank pays upon invocation (financial) / after verifying default (performance).
  • Bank debits borrower's account; if insufficient, debits CC/OD.
  • If still default: recovery as per loan account rules.

Non-Fund Based Limit (NFBL)

  • LoG + LC fall under NFBL.
  • Appraisal includes borrower's TOL/TNW, operating cycle.
  • Within bank's exposure to borrower.
5PSB Reforms — EASE 7.0
▸

EASE = Enhanced Access and Service Excellence. PSB Reforms Agenda launched 2018.

Evolution

  • EASE 1.0 (2018-19): Responsible banking, financial inclusion.
  • EASE 2.0 (2019-20): Technology-enabled ease.
  • EASE 3.0 (2020-21): Digital transformation.
  • EASE 4.0 (2021-22): Smart banking.
  • EASE 5.0 (2022-23): Inclusive customer-centric banking.
  • EASE 6.0 (2023-24): Digital, HR, risk.
  • EASE 7.0 (2024-25): Current — emphasizing cyber security, risk governance, digital-first.

Six Themes of EASE 6.0 / 7.0 (illustrative)

  1. Responsible Banking.
  2. Customer Responsiveness.
  3. Credit Off-Take.
  4. PSBs as Udyami Mitra for MSMEs.
  5. Deepening Financial Inclusion & Digitalisation.
  6. Governance & HR.

Key Initiatives

  • Digital lending platforms.
  • End-to-end digitization of customer journeys.
  • Real-time analytics-driven decisioning.
  • Co-lending frameworks.
  • Sustainability / ESG integration.
6NSFDC — National SC Finance Dev Corporation
▸
  • Established 1989 under Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment.
  • Apex corporation for financing SCs.

Target Beneficiaries

  • SC community members.
  • Income cap: Below Poverty Line (DPL) + 2× DPL.

Schemes

  • Term Loan: up to ₹30 lakh (viable projects).
  • Mahila Samridhi Yojana: ₹2.5 lakh for SC women.
  • Micro Credit Finance: ₹50,000.
  • Education Loan: ₹30 lakh (abroad) / ₹20 lakh (domestic).
  • Mahila Samriddhi: ₹50,000 micro finance for SC women.

Interest Rates

  • 4% to 8% depending on amount + scheme.
  • Concessional rates lower than market.

Channel

  • State Channelising Agencies (SCAs): SC Finance Corporations at state level.
  • Banks as Channel Partners (CPs).
  • Co-op banks, NBFCs also partner.
7Valuation of Land & Building
▸

Valuation Methods

  1. Market Comparison (Direct Comparison): Compared with recent sales of similar properties in the area.
  2. Depreciated Replacement Cost (DRC): Cost of similar construction less depreciation + land value.
  3. Rental Income Capitalization: Annual rent ÷ Yield rate.
  4. Development Method: For land awaiting development.

Valuer Qualifications

  • Civil Engineer (Degree + 5 years) / Architect.
  • IBBI-registered valuer (under Companies Act).
  • Empanelled with bank.
  • Minimum experience as per bank policy.

IBA Format Report Contents

  • Property details (location, type, area).
  • Title details (sale deed, encumbrances).
  • Market analysis (recent sales in area).
  • Valuation workings.
  • Reasonable fair market value + distress / realization value.
  • Photos + measurements.
  • Any encroachment / dispute.

Validity

  • Typically 3 years for housing loan.
  • Fresh valuation for enhancement / re-visit.

Legal Scrutiny

  • 30 years back title chain minimum.
  • EC (Encumbrance Certificate): 13 years (standard); 30 years (for high-value).
  • Non-Encumbrance Certificate.
  • No pending litigation.
  • Title clear and marketable.
  • Done by empanelled lawyer.

Two Valuations for High-Value Property

  • Single property > ₹5 Cr: two valuations.
  • > ₹50 Cr: one from corporate/reputed firm + additional legal opinion.
8Co-Lending Model
▸
  • RBI Framework 2020 — collaboration between Banks + NBFCs.
  • Jointly lend to priority sector (initially; now broader).
  • Bank: 80% share; NBFC: 20% (indicative).
  • Rate: blended; benefits both lender and borrower.
  • Portfolio-wise or account-wise arrangements.
  • KYC + PSL target benefits.
  • Outsourcing of sourcing + servicing to NBFC.
9Priority Sector Lending Certificates (PSLC)
▸
  • Tradable instrument representing PSL achievement.
  • Traded on RBI eKuber platform.
  • 4 types:
    • PSLC — General
    • PSLC — Agriculture
    • PSLC — SF/MF (Small & Marginal Farmers)
    • PSLC — Micro
  • Minimum lot: ₹25 lakh.
  • Validity: till 31 March of issue year (financial year).
  • Buyer counts for PSL achievement; Seller gets premium income.
10Large Exposure Framework (LEF)
▸
  • RBI Large Exposure Framework — effective 2019.
  • Replaces older Exposure Norms.

Key Limits

  • Single Counterparty: 20% of Tier 1 Capital.
  • Group of Connected Counterparties: 25% of Tier 1 Capital.
  • In exceptional cases: additional 5% with Board approval (total 25%/30%).
  • Large exposure = ≥ 10% of Tier 1 Capital.
  • Sum of all large exposures: ≤ 800% of Tier 1 Capital.

Reporting

  • Quarterly to RBI.
  • Monitored by Risk Management + Board.
11Other Important Points
▸

Banking Ombudsman — Historical Context

  • Older schemes replaced by Integrated Ombudsman Scheme 2021.

Banking Codes & Standards Board of India (BCSBI)

  • Set up 2006; wound up 2020-21.
  • Role absorbed by RBI/IBA.

Narasimham Committee Recommendations (I & II)

  • Committee I (1991): BR reforms, SLR/CRR reduction, prudential norms.
  • Committee II (1998): Further recommendations on NPA, provisioning, Basel.

Key Regulators

  • RBI: Central Banking, monetary policy, regulation.
  • SEBI: Securities market.
  • IRDAI: Insurance.
  • PFRDA: Pension (NPS/APY).
  • FSDC: Financial Stability & Development Council (apex).

Digital Banking Framework

  • RBI Digital Lending Guidelines 2022.
  • LSP (Loan Service Provider) regulation.
  • Cooling-off period for digital loans.
  • Transparent APR disclosure.
