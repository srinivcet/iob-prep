1FEMA 1999
▸

Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 — replaced FERA 1973.

Features

  • Objective: Facilitate external trade + promote orderly development of forex market.
  • Civil law (vs FERA which was criminal).
  • Effective June 2000.
  • Administered by: RBI (forex) + Dept of Economic Affairs + DGFT (trade policy).

Current vs Capital Account Transactions

  • Current Account: Generally allowed (import/export, travel, education, medical, gifts).
  • Capital Account: Regulated — permitted only as per FEMA regulations.

Resident vs Non-Resident

  • Resident: In India > 182 days in preceding FY.
  • Non-Resident: Otherwise.
  • FEMA definition differs from Income Tax Act.

Authorised Dealer (AD) Categories

  • AD Cat-I: Full range of forex transactions (main banks).
  • AD Cat-II: Money changing + specified non-trade current.
  • AD Cat-III: Full-fledged money changers.

Key Agencies

  • FEDAI: Foreign Exchange Dealers' Association of India.
  • Enforcement Directorate (ED): Investigates FEMA contraventions.
  • CBIC: Customs.
2Accounts by Resident Indians (Foreign Currency)
▸

EEFC — Exchange Earners' Foreign Currency

  • For: Exporters, foreign exchange earners.
  • Can credit 100% of forex earnings.
  • Current account — no interest.
  • Can use for own imports / permissible uses.

RFC — Resident Foreign Currency

  • For: Returning NRIs.
  • Funds inherit character from NRE/FCNR.
  • Interest tax-free initially (for 1 year of return).

RFC(D) — Resident Foreign Currency (Domestic)

  • Residents can hold forex received as:
    • Gifts, inheritance, honorarium.
    • Unused forex from foreign travel.
  • Current account; no interest.

DDA — Diamond Dollar Account

  • Diamond exporters with turnover > ₹3 Cr.
  • Max 5 accounts per firm.
3Accounts by Non-Residents
▸

NRE — Non-Resident External Rupee Account

  • Currency: INR. Fully repatriable.
  • Source: Foreign inward only.
  • Tax-free (principal + interest).
  • Types: SB, CA, FD, RD.
  • TD tenure: 1-10 years.
  • Joint: With another NRI OR resident close relative on E/S only.

NRO — Non-Resident Ordinary

  • Currency: INR. For Indian income (rent, pension, dividends).
  • Repatriation: USD 1 million / FY for principal (current income freely repatriable).
  • Interest: TDS @ 30%.
  • Can be joint with NRI or Resident on E/S or F/S.

FCNR(B) — Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank)

  • Currencies: USD, GBP, EUR, JPY, CAD, AUD.
  • Only Term Deposit: 1-5 years.
  • Tax-free, fully repatriable, exchange-risk immune for depositor.
  • Joint: With NRI only (variant allows close relative resident).

SNRR — Special Non-Resident Rupee

  • For specific business: trade, investment, ECB.
  • Max 7 years tenure; no interest.
4Vostro / Nostro / Loro Accounts
▸
  • Nostro: "Our account" — Indian bank's account maintained with foreign bank (in foreign currency).
  • Vostro: "Your account" — Foreign bank's account maintained with Indian bank (in INR).
  • Loro: "Their account" — third party's account.

Special Rupee Vostro Account (SRVA)

  • For international trade settlement in INR.
  • Permits use of INR as settlement currency in cross-border trade.
  • RBI framework July 2022.

Reconciliation

  • Nostro reconciliation: daily/weekly.
  • Monthly R-Returns to RBI (summary of forex business).
5FEDAI Rules (10th Edition)
▸

Foreign Exchange Dealers' Association of India — self-regulatory body for AD banks.

Key Rules

  • Forex business hours: 9 AM to 5 PM (interbank).
  • Cash: Same day settlement.
  • TOM: Next business day settlement.
  • Spot: T+2 (standard) settlement.
  • Forward: Beyond spot.

Export Bill Handling

  • Purchase vs Negotiation: Different handling.
  • Bills on crystallisation: due date + 30 days if not realised.

Import Bill Handling

  • Payment on due date.
  • Crystallisation: after 10 days of due date (if not paid).

Charges

  • Flat rate for small transactions.
  • Negotiated for larger.
6Export of Goods & Services
▸

Governing Framework

  • FEMA 1999 + Master Directions on Export of Goods and Services.
  • Foreign Trade Policy (FTP).
  • Customs Act.

Export Documentation

  • EDF (Export Declaration Form): Replaced GR Form; required for goods.
  • SOFTEX: For software/services export.
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill.
  • Commercial Invoice.
  • Packing List.
  • Certificate of Origin.
  • Insurance policy.
  • Letter of Credit (if applicable).

Realisation Period

  • Goods: 9 months from date of export.
  • Software/services: 9 months.
  • SEZ/EOU: Specific extensions may apply.

EDPMS

  • Export Data Processing and Monitoring System — RBI portal.
  • Tracks: Shipment vs Realisation.
  • Non-realisation > 2 years → Caution List.

Caution List

  • Non-realisation of export proceeds > 2 years = Caution.
  • Bank to inform exporter.
  • Future exports require advance payment / LC.
  • De-cautioning on realisation or satisfactory explanation.
7Export Credit (Packing Credit)
▸

Pre-Shipment Credit

  • "Packing Credit" — working capital for export processing.
  • Against: LC, confirmed order, firm export contract.
  • Tenure: Max 180 days (extendable 90 days).
  • Rate: Concessional (RLLR - spread).
  • Must be liquidated from export proceeds.

Post-Shipment Credit

  • After shipment, before realisation.
  • Types:
    • Bills Purchased / Discounted.
    • Advance against collection.
    • Export bills negotiated under LC.
  • Tenure: 6 months (goods); 9 months with RBI extension.

Interest Equalisation Scheme (IES)

  • Up to 3% for all MSME exports.
  • 2% for non-MSME in specified HS codes (identified sectors).
  • Reduces effective interest to exporter.

PCFC — Packing Credit in Foreign Currency

  • At LIBOR/SOFR + spread.
  • Avoids forex risk for borrower.
8Import of Goods & Services
▸

Required Documents

  • IEC (Importer Exporter Code): 10-digit from DGFT.
  • Bill of Entry (BoE): Customs proof.
  • Commercial Invoice.
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill.
  • Letter of Credit (if applicable).
  • Insurance.

Payment Timeline

  • Within 6 months of shipment (extendable by RBI).
  • Advance Remittance: up to USD 2 lakh without BG.
  • Above USD 2 lakh: BG from international bank required.
  • Advance for services: up to USD 5,00,000 without BG.

IDPMS

  • Import Data Processing and Monitoring System — RBI portal.
  • Tracks BoE submission + payment.

Merchanting Trade

  • Both legs (export + import) are by Indian resident, goods don't enter India.
  • Complete within 9 months.
  • Export value ≥ Import value.
9UCPDC 600 (Letter of Credit)
▸

Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits — ICC Publication 600 (effective 2007).

Features

  • 39 Articles.
  • Standard rules for LC operations worldwide.
  • Strict compliance required — discrepancies lead to dishonour.

Key Articles

  • Art 2: Definitions.
  • Art 3: Interpretations.
  • Art 4: Credit vs Contract — LC is separate from underlying contract.
  • Art 5: Documents, not goods.
  • Art 14: Standard for examination of documents (5 banking days).
  • Art 16: Discrepant documents, waiver, notice.
  • Art 29: Extension of last day for presentation.
  • Art 38: Transferable Credits (one transfer only; original + 1).
  • Art 39: Assignment of proceeds.

Timelines

  • Documents must be presented within 21 days of shipment date OR by LC expiry date (whichever earlier).
  • Banks: 5 banking days to examine documents.

Types of LC

  • Revocable vs Irrevocable: Default is Irrevocable under UCPDC 600.
  • Confirmed vs Unconfirmed: Confirming bank adds its undertaking.
  • Sight vs Usance (Term): Sight = immediate payment; Usance = deferred.
  • Revolving LC: Automatically reinstates after use.
  • Red Clause LC: Advance to beneficiary.
  • Green Clause: Advance + storage financing.
  • Back-to-Back LC: Second LC based on first LC as collateral.
  • Standby LC: Similar to BG, backing performance/payment.

11LRS — Liberalised Remittance Scheme
▸
  • For resident individuals (incl. minors via guardian).
  • Annual limit: USD 2.5 lakh per financial year.

Permitted Purposes

  • Private travel (except Nepal, Bhutan).
  • Gift / donation.
  • Employment abroad.
  • Emigration.
  • Maintenance of close relatives abroad.
  • Education abroad.
  • Medical treatment abroad.
  • Investment in shares / debt / immovable property abroad.

Not Permitted

  • Lottery tickets / sweepstakes.
  • Foreign exchange trading / stock speculation.
  • Transactions with FATF-designated non-cooperative countries.
  • Remittance for purchase of FCCBs of Indian cos abroad.

12Foreign Trade Policy (FTP) 2023
▸
  • Effective 1 April 2023; ongoing.
  • Replaces FTP 2015-20.

Thrust Areas

  • Trade Facilitation (ease of doing business).
  • E-commerce exports.
  • Districts as Export Hubs.
  • Streamlining SCOMET (dual-use goods).

Key Schemes

  • RoDTEP: Remission of Duties and Taxes on Exported Products.
  • EPCG: Export Promotion Capital Goods — 0% customs duty on capital goods with export obligation = 6× duty saved in 6 years.
  • Advance Authorisation: Duty-free import of inputs with export obligation.
  • DFIA: Duty Free Import Authorisation.
  • Towns of Export Excellence.

Status Holder

  • Star Export House based on export value.
  • 1 Star / 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 Stars.
  • Privileges: self-certification, ease of procedures, concessional fees.

14Derivatives & Forex Risk Hedging
▸

Types of Derivatives

  • Forward: OTC, custom-tailored, no exchange.
  • Futures: Exchange-traded, standardised.
  • Options: Right (not obligation) to buy/sell.
  • Swaps: Exchange of cash flows (IRS, CCS).

Currency Derivatives in India

  • Exchange-traded: NSE, BSE, MCX-SX.
  • Pairs: USD/INR, EUR/INR, GBP/INR, JPY/INR, Cross-currency pairs.

Hedging Products

  • Forward Rate Agreement (FRA).
  • Currency Swaps (Cross Currency Swap - CCS).
  • Interest Rate Swaps (IRS).
  • Options.

RBI Rules

  • Hedging allowed for corporates with underlying exposure.
  • Speculative positions restricted.
  • USD/INR pair: contract size ~USD 1,000 (exchange-traded).
15External Commercial Borrowings (ECB)
▸
  • Borrowings in forex by Indian entities.
  • Sources: International banks, capital markets, suppliers, foreign parent.
  • Framework: Automatic Route (up to sectoral caps) + Approval Route.

Key Limits

  • Automatic Route: up to USD 750 million (manufacturing, services).
  • Specific sectors: Different caps.
  • Minimum Average Maturity (MAM): 3 years (generally).
  • All-in-Cost Ceiling: Benchmark (SOFR) + 450 bps for most.

End-Use Restrictions

  • Not for: Real estate, capital market, equity investment in subsidiaries.
  • Permitted: Capex, working capital (restrictions apply), refinancing existing ECB.
